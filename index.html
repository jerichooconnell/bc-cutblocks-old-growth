Definitions. "Old growth" is any VRI stand aged >150 yr on forest-capable land in CWH/CDF/ICH — no productivity requirement. Site index instead sets each stand's productivity tier relative to its own BEC zone's full forest-capable land base (per-zone 75th/85th/95th percentile cutoffs: –), the same tiering used on the companion large-stature old-growth map, which — unlike this one — does use that 75th-percentile floor to define what counts as "old growth" in the first place. Proposed cutblocks come from BC's Forest Operations Map (FOM) — the advance public-notice layer forest companies must post before operations begin — filtered to LIFECYCLE_STATUS = "Proposed" (as opposed to "Final"), and matched against currently standing old growth. An earlier version of this map used FTEN tenure records instead, but that layer's status codes turned out to be an unreliable "not yet logged" signal; FOM's status field is unambiguous. A cutblock touching more than one old-growth stand is tagged with the highest site index it overlaps.

Statement of Intent boundaries are the traditional-territory boundaries First Nations have registered with the BC Treaty Commission to enter treaty negotiations — a claim/negotiation area, not a legal land designation, and boundaries frequently overlap between neighbouring nations. Shown for geographic context only; off by default.

How to use this map

This map tracks proposed BC cutblocks that overlap standing old growth in the Coastal Western Hemlock (CWH), Coastal Douglas-fir (CDF), and Interior Cedar-Hemlock (ICH) biogeoclimatic zones. It's built to help you find a specific proposal near you, understand how significant it is, and take concrete action while its public comment window is still open.

Features

  • Find an area — search a town or place, or tap "Near me" to jump to the cutblocks around your location.
  • Search & filter (left panel) — search by cutblock name or licensee, and filter by BEC zone, NR region, NR district, TFL, and productivity tier. The list and the map always show the same set of cutblocks.
  • Recently posted — a "new"/"recent" badge and a filter surface the cutblocks posted in the last 30 or 90 days — the ones whose public comment window is most likely still open. Sort by "most recently posted" to put the freshest at the top.
  • Productivity tier — site index (a proxy for how big/valuable the trees on a stand can get) buckets each old-growth stand into a tier relative to its own BEC zone: 0 = below the 75th percentile, up to 3 = 95th percentile and above. Darker/more saturated cutblock color = higher tier. Use this to prioritize the rarest, highest-productivity stands.
  • Click any cutblock (on the map or in the list) for its exact site index, licensee, planned area, posted/planned-development dates, region, district, TFL, a direct link to comment on it, and a "copy link" button so you can share that exact block.
  • Share & download — the page URL captures your current filters, so you can send someone a specific view; the CSV / GeoJSON buttons export exactly the cutblocks you're looking at.
  • Map layers — toggle protected areas (parks, conservancies, national parks) to see what's already protected, and Statement of Intent boundaries to see whose traditional territory a cutblock falls within.
  • Data updates weekly — proposed cutblocks are refreshed every Monday from BC's Forest Operations Map, so newly posted proposals and expired ones stay current.

Take action

These are listed roughly in order of how directly they connect to a specific cutblock on this map. All contact details below are current as of when this page was last updated — always confirm on the linked government page, since staff and contact details do change.

1. Comment on the specific proposed cutblock

Every proposed cutblock has a legally required public review-and-comment period (typically 30 days from posting) on BC's official Forest Operations Map portal. Comments go directly to the plan holder and Ministry of Forests staff. Click a cutblock on this map for its FOM ID, then search that ID on the portal.

fom.nrs.gov.bc.ca/public/projects ↗

2. Contact the Natural Resource District office

Each cutblock's popup shows its NR District — that district office administers the tenure and is the most direct government contact for a specific operating area.

District office directory ↗

3. Reach out to the Treaty First Nation whose territory is affected

Turn on the Statement of Intent layer to see whose traditional territory a cutblock falls within. The BC Treaty Commission's own interactive map links out to individual First Nations for further information and contact details.

BC Treaty Commission interactive map ↗ · BC Treaty Commission contact ↗

604-482-9200 · info@bctreaty.ca

4. File a concern with the BC Forest Practices Board

The Board is government-funded but independent, and investigates public complaints about forest planning, harvesting practices, and resource protection (water, viewscapes, recreation) — including whether government enforcement itself was adequate.

File a concern ↗

1-800-994-5899 (BC toll-free) · fpbcomplaints@bcfpb.ca

5. Contact the Ministry of Forests / Office of the Minister

For policy-level concerns — e.g. questioning whether old growth this significant should be available for harvest at all — the Minister's office and general public-inquiry line are the province-wide contact.
